How to Speak Pig Latin

Pig Latin sounds ridiculous—which is exactly why it’s fun. Once you know a few simple rules, you can start turning regular English into Pig Latin almost immediately.

🐷 Pig Latin Is Easier Than It Looks

Pig Latin is a word game that changes English words by moving sounds around and adding an ending.

  • You don’t need a new alphabet.
  • You don’t need to memorize hundreds of words.
  • You just need to learn a few patterns.
1

Rule #1: Move It!

If a word starts with a consonant, take every consonant before the first vowel, move that chunk to the end of the word, and add ay. One letter or a whole cluster like sch or tr moves together.

  • pig→igpay
  • latin→atinlay
  • hello→ellohay
  • school→oolschay
  • train→aintray

Pink = the sound that moves. Underlined = the ending you add.

2

Rule #2: Vowel Words Get It Easy

If a word starts with a vowel (a, e, i, o, or u), keep the whole word and add YAY in the Northern dialect or WAY in the Southern dialect.

  • orange→orangeyay
  • egg→eggyay
  • apple→appleyay(Northern)
  • apple→appleway(Southern)

🐷 The Great YAY vs. WAY Debate

Pig Latin has more than one version. Some people add YAY to words that begin with a vowel, while others add WAY. So orange can become orangeyay or orangeway.

Both are Pig Latin.

Even Piglatinia has regional differences! Northern Piglatinia traditionally uses YAY for vowel-starting words (apple → appleyay), while Southern Piglatinia traditionally uses WAY (apple → appleway).

LearnPigLatin.com usually teaches the YAY version—but if you learned WAY, you’re speaking Pig Latin too.

What About the Letter Y?

Y plays both sides. At the start of a word, Y is a consonant, so it moves like any other consonant:

  • yes→esyay
  • yellow→ellowyay

Anywhere else in a word, Y acts like a vowel, so it stays put and everything before it moves:

  • my→ymay
  • gym→ymgay

🗣️ Now Say It!

Reading Pig Latin slowly is the easy part. Speaking it quickly is where it gets fun.

  1. Start slowly.
  2. Say the Pig Latin version out loud.
  3. Speed it up.
  4. Try it without thinking about the rule.

Can you say it three times fast?

Igspay ovelay itterglay

(That’s “Pigs love glitter.” Piglatinians say it on the hoop-powered bus to Oopapalishay every morning.)
